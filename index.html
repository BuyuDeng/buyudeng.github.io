Selected Projects

CO₂ Storage Modeling and Rock Physics Agent Development

At Stanford SCERF, I compared five grid resolutions in ECLIPSE 300 CO₂STORE simulations using fixed-time plume geometry, fixed-location saturation histories, space-time plume metrics, and computational cost. I selected a 0.12 ft vertical grid for subsequent modeling.

I mapped geology and CO₂ saturation to elastic properties with a facies-based rock physics workflow, then generated baseline and time-lapse seismic responses for 20 years of injection and 80 years after shut-in.

I also designed an agent workflow using the Stanford Rock Physics and Borehole Geophysics (SRB) model library. Across 45 independent runs, I compared agents with no references, textbook guidance, and textbook guidance plus SRB tools. Textbook guidance recovered the prescribed physics and reference results; tools enabled direct model calls but still required careful interpretation of outputs.

Geo‑VAE

Geo‑VAE adapts the pretrained Wan‑VAE to map seismic, impedance, RGT, fault masks, and field seismic into a compact latent domain. Cached causal resampling treats crossline as a pseudo-temporal sequence, yielding isotropic 8×8×8 downsampling and 31:1 scalar compression for 256³ cubes.

Across five 3D geophysical modalities, it achieves strong reconstruction fidelity and a favorable balance of GPU memory use and inference time among tested state-of-the-art video VAEs on 128³–512³ cubes. Its unified compact latent space supports RGT-conditioned seismic generation and complete-volume 768³ denoising.
